Find a drop-off or recycling site

Food banks, pantries and soup kitchens take safe surplus food. Composting and food-waste recycling sites take scraps. Distances are straight-line from the selected county’s center or from your location. Turn site types on or off below.

    Most sites come from the EPA Excess Food Opportunities Map (2025). A few hand-entered sites (waste-to-energy plants, landfills, WM CORe and Center for Food Action) have approximate locations. Confirm hours and what each site accepts before you go.

    Ways to keep food out of the trash

    Am I a “large food waste generator”?

    Under New Jersey’s Food Waste Recycling Law (P.L. 2020, c.24), businesses and institutions that make at least 52 tons of food waste a year must separate it and send it to an authorized recycling facility if one is within 25 road miles.

    Progress toward the 2030 goal

    New Jersey’s Food Waste Reduction Act (2017) sets a goal of cutting food waste 50% by 2030.
